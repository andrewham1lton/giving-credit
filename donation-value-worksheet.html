A value worksheet for donated goods

When you drop a bag at Goodwill or the Salvation Army, the receipt usually leaves the value blank. Putting a figure on each item is your job, and the IRS asks for fair market value: roughly what the item would sell for, used, in its condition. Thrift and consignment shop prices are one indication; there is no fixed formula.

Write the list the day you give, while you still know what was in the bag. Print this page, or copy the columns below into a spreadsheet.

Filling it in

  • Condition. Clothing and household items count only in good used condition or better. A worn-out shirt has no value to record.
  • Guide range. Look the item up in a charity’s guide (links below). The guides assume good condition; pick a figure inside the range for the item’s age and quality.
  • How you chose it. A few words are enough: “Salvation Army guide, nearly new”. The IRS asks for the method you used, and the note is how you’ll remember it in April.

When a bag needs more than a list

  • Under $250 for one drop-off: keep the charity’s receipt, or your own written record of each item where a receipt isn’t practical.
  • $250 or more: you need the charity’s written acknowledgment. See what a $250 letter must say.
  • Over $500 of goods for the year: Form 8283 Section A. See what Section A asks for.

Where Giving Credit fits

Giving Credit keeps this worksheet on your iPhone: photograph the drop-off, pick each item from a guide of nearly 200, and see its range with the charity guides it comes from. A suggested value stays in pencil and isn’t counted until you confirm it.
