A year-end checklist for your charity records

Work through this in December, while gifts are still fresh, and again in January when the letters arrive. Most lines link to more detail.

Money gifts

  • Every gift has a bank record or a receipt with the charity’s name, the date and the amount. What counts
  • Every single gift of $250 or more has the charity’s written acknowledgment, and you have it before you file. What it must say
  • Any gift to a donor-advised fund is marked, if you take the standard deduction: it doesn’t count toward the $1,000 or $2,000 limit.

Donated goods

  • Each drop-off has a receipt and a list of what was in it, with a value and how you chose it. Value worksheet
  • Clothing and household items were in good used condition or better.
  • Any drop-off of $250 or more has a written acknowledgment.
  • If goods come to more than $500 for the year, you have the Form 8283 Section A details. What Section A asks for
  • Any item or group of similar items over $5,000 has a qualified appraisal. Over $5,000

Volunteering

  • Your mileage log shows the date, charity and miles for each trip, plus parking and tolls. Mileage at 14 cents

Handing it over

  • You know whether you’ll itemize or take the standard deduction; ask your preparer if you’re unsure.
  • One summary with your totals for money, goods and mileage, and the receipts and letters behind them, in one folder.
  • If you use TurboTax Desktop, the totals are ready to import. Importing a TXF file

Where Giving Credit fits

Giving Credit works through most of this list for you: every gift shows its proof status, the year shows what still needs a letter or a value, and the year-end summary is a PDF and a CSV, with Form 8283 filled in from your confirmed goods.
