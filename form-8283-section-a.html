Form 8283 Section A: what it asks for, and when it applies

Form 8283 is the IRS form for noncash charitable contributions: clothes, furniture, cars, art, stock. You file it with your return if you deduct more than $500 for a noncash contribution, and the IRS counts a group of similar items together.

When it applies

  • Over $500 of deduction for donated goods: Form 8283 is filed with the return.
  • $5,000 or less per item or group of similar items: Section A.
  • More than $5,000 per item or group: Section B, which needs a qualified appraisal. See gifts over $500 and over $5,000.
  • Not on Form 8283: money you gave by cash, check or card, and out-of-pocket costs of volunteering. Those are cash contributions.

“Similar items” means things of the same general kind: clothing, books, furniture, paintings. All the clothing you gave in a year is one group, even across several charities.

What Section A asks for

For each donated item, or each group of similar items, Section A wants:

  • the charity’s name and address
  • a description of the property
  • the date you gave it
  • the approximate date you acquired it
  • how you acquired it: bought, gift, inheritance or exchange
  • your cost or other basis
  • its fair market value
  • the method you used to find that value

Cost is required for property you held less than 12 months; for property held longer, give it if you have it. The form also asks for your name and taxpayer identification number.

Getting ready for it

  • Keep one list per drop-off: date, charity, items and the value of each. A value worksheet does the job.
  • For anything worth over $500 on its own, write down when and how you got it, and what you paid, while you can still find the receipt.
  • Note the method for each value, for example “charity value guide” or “thrift store price for the same item”.
