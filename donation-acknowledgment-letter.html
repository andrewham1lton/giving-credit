What a $250 donation letter must say

For any single gift of $250 or more, the law asks for a written acknowledgment from the charity. A bank statement or canceled check isn’t enough on its own for these gifts. The rule is the same for money and for donated goods.

What the letter must say

  • The amount of cash you gave, or for goods, a description of what you gave. The charity describes goods but doesn’t put a value on them; the value is yours.
  • Whether the charity gave you anything in return, such as a dinner, a ticket or merchandise.
  • If it did, a description and a good-faith estimate of what that was worth. If the only thing you received was an intangible religious benefit, the letter says so instead.

When you need it by

The letter has to be in your hands by the day you file your return, or by the return’s due date including extensions, whichever comes first.

$250 is per gift

$250.00 needs a letter; $249.99 doesn’t. Separate gifts aren’t added together to reach $250, so twelve monthly gifts of $50 don’t need one, though a single $300 gift does.

Asking for one

If a charity hasn’t sent one, you can ask with a short email:

Hello, I gave [amount or description of items] on [date]. Could you send me a written acknowledgment for my tax records, saying whether I received any goods or services in return? Thank you, [your name and address].

Where Giving Credit fits

Giving Credit marks every gift of $250 or more “letter needed” until you have the charity’s letter, and drafts the request for you to send from your own Mail.
