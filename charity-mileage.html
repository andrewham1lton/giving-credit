Charity mileage: 14 cents a mile

If you drive your own car to volunteer for a charity, the miles have a value you can record: 14 cents a mile. The rate is set by law, not by the IRS’s yearly cost study, so it doesn’t change when the IRS revises its business and medical rates.

Working out a trip

Miles times $0.14, rounded to the cent:

TripMilesValue
Food bank shift, round trip18$2.52
Weekly meal deliveries42$5.88
Driving to a charity conference120$16.80

Parking fees and tolls for the trip can be added on top of the mileage figure.

What your log should show

The IRS asks for reliable written records. At the 14-cent rate, they must show the miles you drove for the charity. A useful log has, for each trip:

  • the date
  • the charity, and what you were doing for it
  • where you drove, and the miles
  • any parking or tolls, with receipts

Write it down on the day; a log rebuilt in April from memory is hard to rely on. If a single unreimbursed expense comes to $250 or more, you also need records of it and a written acknowledgment from the charity.

Mileage is recorded with your itemized gifts. If you take the standard deduction, ask your preparer how it’s treated.

Where Giving Credit fits

Giving Credit records volunteer miles with the date and the charity, values each trip at 14 cents a mile and rounds it to the cent. It keeps mileage on its own line in your year, apart from your cash gifts.
